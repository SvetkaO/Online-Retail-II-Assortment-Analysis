# Online Retail II — Assortment Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the product assortment of a UK online gift and home retailer.

The company has a large number of SKUs, including products with very low or irregular sales. Managing a large assortment can increase inventory complexity and storage costs.

The business is considering reducing the assortment by approximately **20%**, but the goal is not to remove products only to reach this target. The main objective is to identify products that can be reviewed for possible removal with a low risk to revenue.

## Business Questions

The analysis answers the following questions:

* How concentrated is revenue across the assortment?
* How are products distributed between ABC and XYZ groups?
* Which product groups are most important for the business?
* Which products should have regular stock availability?
* Which products require more careful inventory management?
* Which SKUs can be considered for assortment reduction?
* What share of the assortment can be reviewed for removal?
* What share of historical revenue was generated by these products?
* What are the main risks of the recommendation?

## Data

The project uses the **Online Retail II** dataset with transaction-level data.

The main fields used in the analysis are:

* Invoice
* StockCode
* Description
* Quantity
* InvoiceDate
* UnitPrice
* CustomerID
* Country

The final analysis covers **January 2010 – December 2011**, giving two complete consecutive years of sales history.

### Data Quality

The dataset contains several data quality issues:

* missing `CustomerID` and `Description`;
* cancelled invoices;
* negative quantities;
* duplicate rows;
* inconsistent StockCode formatting;
* accounting adjustments that are not real product sales.

Only actual sales were used for the assortment analysis:

* `Quantity > 0`;
* cancelled invoices were excluded;
* StockCodes were standardized;
* duplicate rows were removed;
* accounting adjustment `StockCode B — Adjust bad debt` was excluded because it does not represent a real product sale.

Missing `CustomerID` values were not removed because the main analysis is performed at SKU level.

## Analysis Approach

The analysis was completed in several steps.

### 1. Business Performance

The first stage measured the main business indicators:

* number of unique SKUs;
* number of orders;
* number of identified customers;
* units sold;
* revenue;
* average order value;
* monthly order and revenue dynamics.

Revenue was calculated as:

`Revenue = Quantity × UnitPrice`

### 2. ABC Analysis

Products were classified based on their contribution to total revenue:

* **A** — products generating approximately the first 80% of revenue;
* **B** — products generating the next 15%;
* **C** — products generating the remaining 5%.

The analysis shows strong revenue concentration:

* A products represent about **20% of SKUs** and generate about **80% of revenue**;
* B products represent about **25% of SKUs** and generate about **15% of revenue**;
* C products represent about **55% of SKUs** and generate only about **5% of revenue**.

### 3. XYZ Analysis

XYZ analysis was based on the coefficient of variation of monthly sales.

A complete 24-month calendar was created for every SKU, including months with zero sales.

Products were classified as:

* **X** — relatively stable demand;
* **Y** — moderately variable demand;
* **Z** — highly variable demand.

Most products belong to category Z.

However, **Z should not automatically be interpreted as unpredictable customer demand**. High variation can also result from rare sales, seasonality, a short product history, or periods when the product was unavailable.

### 4. ABC/XYZ Matrix

ABC and XYZ classifications were combined to understand both the financial importance and sales stability of each SKU.

The most important groups are:

* **AX** — high revenue contribution and relatively stable sales;
* **AY** — high revenue contribution with moderate variability;
* **AZ** — high revenue contribution with high variability;
* **CZ** — low revenue contribution and highly variable sales.

AY generates approximately **35.9% of total revenue**, while AZ generates approximately **31.2%**.

The CZ group is the main area for assortment optimization because it contains many products with a low contribution to revenue.

## Key Findings

1. Revenue is strongly concentrated: around **20% of SKUs generate approximately 80% of total revenue**.

2. More than half of the assortment belongs to category C and generates only about **5% of revenue**.

3. Most products have high monthly sales variability and belong to category Z. However, high variability does not always mean unpredictable demand.

4. **AY and AZ are critical groups**, together generating a large share of total revenue. These products require careful inventory management.

5. **AX products should generally have regular availability**, while AZ products require more careful stock planning because of their high revenue importance and variable sales.

6. The **CZ group** is the main area for possible assortment optimization.

7. A conservative selection identified **369 SKUs**, representing **7.96% of the total assortment**, for further review.

8. These 369 SKUs generated only **0.17% of total historical revenue**.

## Assortment Reduction Recommendation

Products were not selected for reduction only because they belonged to the CZ group.

Additional activity criteria were used.

A product was initially selected if it:

* belonged to the **CZ group**;
* had no more than **9 orders**;
* had no more than **5 identified customers**;
* was active in no more than **4 months**.

These thresholds were based on the first quartile of the metric distributions inside the CZ group.

This initial selection identified **421 low-activity SKUs**.

Two additional checks were then applied:

* **33 new products** were excluded because their first sale occurred during the last three months of the analysis period;
* **19 products with possible signs of seasonality** were excluded because they had repeated sales in the same calendar months in both analyzed years.

The final list contains:

**369 SKUs = 7.96% of the assortment**

These products generated only:

**0.17% of total historical revenue**

Therefore, the recommendation is to **review these 369 SKUs for possible assortment reduction**.

The original business target of approximately 20% was not reached intentionally. Increasing the reduction list to 20% would require less strict criteria and could increase the risk of removing products with meaningful demand.

The analysis therefore supports a more conservative first step of reviewing approximately **8% of the assortment**.

## Inventory Recommendations

Different ABC/XYZ groups require different inventory strategies.

**AX — Maintain availability**

These products are important for revenue and have relatively stable sales. Stock-outs should be avoided.

**AY — Maintain stock and monitor demand**

These products are important for revenue, but sales are less stable. Inventory should be reviewed regularly.

**AZ — Careful inventory planning**

These products generate significant revenue but have highly variable sales. They should remain available, but excessive safety stock may create inventory risk.

**B groups — Controlled inventory**

These products have a medium contribution to revenue. Stock levels should depend on sales stability and operational costs.

**C groups — Conservative inventory**

These products have a low revenue contribution. CZ products with very low activity are the main candidates for manual review and possible assortment reduction.

## Limitations

This analysis is based mainly on historical transaction data. Several important operational variables are not available.

Most importantly, **sales are not the same as true customer demand**.

If a product was out of stock, transaction data will show zero sales even if customers wanted to buy the product. Therefore, low historical sales cannot automatically be interpreted as low customer interest without product availability data.

Before making a real assortment decision, the following additional data should be requested:

* product cost and gross margin;
* current and historical inventory levels;
* out-of-stock periods;
* storage and warehouse costs;
* supplier lead time;
* minimum order quantity;
* product returns;
* product shelf life or expiration date;
* product category and seasonality;
* promotions and marketing activity;
* strategic role of the product in the shopping basket.

Basket-level information is especially important because a low-revenue product may still help generate sales of other products. Removing it could therefore have an indirect effect that cannot be measured using SKU revenue alone.

The seasonality check also has a limitation: only two complete years were analyzed. Repeated sales in the same calendar month were treated as a **possible sign of seasonality**, not as proof of a stable seasonal pattern.

Because these data are not available, the 369 selected SKUs should be treated as **candidates for manual review, not as products that should be automatically removed**.

## Final Conclusion

The analysis shows that the retailer's revenue is highly concentrated in a relatively small part of the assortment.

ABC/XYZ analysis helps separate high-value products that require strong availability from low-value products that can be managed more conservatively.

A data-based and conservative selection identified **369 SKUs (7.96% of the assortment)** that generated only **0.17% of historical revenue**.

These products are recommended for further business review as possible assortment reduction candidates.

Instead of forcing the assortment reduction to the original 20% target, the recommended approach is to start with this lower-risk group and make the final decision only after adding inventory, margin, stock availability, supplier, storage cost, and basket-role data.
